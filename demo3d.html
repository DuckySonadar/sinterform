01  Rows are not equations

      
In the plane, parallel is one scalar. In space the cross product is
         a vector: three rows that can only ever have rank two, because two directions
         differ by two angles. Each constraint declares the rank it carries, so
         redundant still means what it meant.

02  An arc carries a frame

      
One angle became three, so an arc owns seven numbers rather than five. Pass a
         bare number for the rotation and it means about +Z alone — which is exactly the
         2D phi, so a planar sketch lifts across unchanged.

      
A circular arc keeps one freedom that changes nothing: turn its frame about its
         own normal and slide both extents back, and the curve drawn is the one that was
         there. Expect a dof one higher than the shape's.

    

    

      
03  Angles are unsigned

      
A signed angle needs an axis to be signed about, and space does not supply one.
         Angles run 0 to π. At either end the unsigned angle has a kink — say
         parallel or perpendicular there, which is what you meant.

    

    

      
04  A closed loop bounds nothing

      
Not unless it is flat, and closing it does not make it flat. So planarity is
         measured, faces come with the plane they were found in, and an extrusion runs
         along the face's own normal — along Z would be a shear. A wireframe bounds no
         volume at all, which is why wire asks for a radius.
